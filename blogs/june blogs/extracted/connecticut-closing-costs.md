Connecticut closing costs typically run between 2% and 5% of the purchase price — $8,000 to $20,000 on a $400,000 home. That number surprises most first-time buyers. But the bigger surprise is usually the property tax escrow deposit, which can add $5,000–$16,000 on top of everything else depending on which town you buy in.



This guide breaks down every line item on a Connecticut closing disclosure, explains the CT-specific costs that catch buyers off guard, and tells you exactly how much to set aside before you start shopping.

How Much Are Closing Costs in Connecticut?



| Purchase Price | Typical Closing Cost Range |

|---|---|

| $300,000 | $6,000–$15,000 |

| $400,000 | $8,000–$20,000 |

| $500,000–$600,000 | $12,000–$25,000 |

Your cash to close is not just closing costs — it also includes your down payment. Budget for both, separately.



The lower end of each range applies to buyers with simple loans, lower-rate towns, and minimal lender fees. The upper end applies to buyers in high-mill-rate towns (where the property tax escrow is large), larger loan sizes, and lenders with higher fee structures.

The Big Variable: Property Tax Escrow by Town



The single biggest surprise for Connecticut buyers — especially those coming from New York City — is the property tax escrow deposit.



At closing, your lender collects 9–10 months of property taxes upfront to seed your escrow account. In a high-mill-rate town like West Hartford or Hartford, this can be $8,000–$16,000 on top of everything else. In a low-rate Fairfield County town, it might be $3,000.



Tax escrow on a $400,000 home by town:



| Town | Mill Rate | Annual Tax on $400K Home | 10-Month Escrow |

|---|---|---|---|

| Greenwich | 12.041 | ~$3,371/yr | ~$2,809 |

| Westport | 18.62 | ~$5,214/yr | ~$4,345 |

| Norwalk | ~23.50 | ~$6,580/yr | ~$5,483 |

| Glastonbury | 32.83 | ~$9,192/yr | ~$7,660 |

| Middletown | 34.50 | ~$9,660/yr | ~$8,050 |

| West Hartford | 44.78 | ~$12,538/yr | ~$10,448 |

| New Haven | ~46.00 | ~$12,880/yr | ~$10,733 |

| Hartford | 68.95 | ~$19,306/yr | ~$16,088 |



CT homes are assessed at 70% of fair market value. Annual tax = (purchase price × 70%) × mill rate ÷ 1,000.



Two buyers purchasing the same price home in different towns have dramatically different cash requirements at closing — purely because of property tax escrow. A Greenwich buyer needs $2,809 in escrow; a Hartford buyer needs $16,088. That $13,000 difference affects everything from how much you need in savings to whether you qualify for certain programs.

Connecticut-Specific Closing Costs



Connecticut has features that differ from most other states — especially important for buyers relocating from New York:



1. Attorney Fees Are Required

Connecticut is an attorney-closing state. A real estate attorney is not optional — they represent you, handle the title search, review all documents, and manage the deed recording. Budget $1,000–$2,000.



2. Recording Goes Through Town Clerks, Not a County

Connecticut has no county recording system. Each of the 169 towns has its own town clerk office. Recording fees vary by town but typically run $150–$200 total.



3. Conveyance Tax Is the Seller's Cost, Not Yours

Connecticut charges a real estate conveyance tax — but it's the seller's obligation. As a buyer, you don't pay it. Just know it exists for when you eventually sell:

State: 0.75% up to $800K; 1.25% from $800K–$2.5M; 2.25% above $2.5M

Municipal: 0.25% (most towns) or 0.50% (some towns)



4. No Mortgage Recording Tax

This one is important for NYC buyers: Connecticut has no mortgage recording tax. New York State charges 1%–2% of the loan amount as a recording tax — on a $500,000 loan in New York, that's $5,000–$10,000. Connecticut charges nothing. This is one of several meaningful savings for buyers relocating from the New York market.

What NYC Buyers Don't Realize About Connecticut Closing Costs



For buyers moving from New York City, Connecticut closing costs feel large at first — until you compare them to what you were facing in New York.



| Cost Item | Connecticut | New York State |

|---|---|---|

| Mortgage recording tax | None | 1%–2% of loan amount |

| Mansion tax (purchases over $1M) | None | 1%–3.9% of purchase price |

| Attorney fee | $1,000–$2,000 | $1,500–$3,000 |

| Title insurance | Negotiable, competitive | Set by rate schedule |

| Conveyance tax (buyer pays) | None | N/A (seller pays in CT too) |



On a $700,000 purchase, a New York buyer pays roughly $7,000–$14,000 in mortgage recording tax alone — which doesn't exist in Connecticut. The total closing cost picture in CT often comes out comparable or lower than NY despite superficially similar ranges.

How to Reduce Your Closing Costs



Use CHFA's Time To Own. CHFA's forgivable down payment assistance covers up to 5% of closing costs in addition to your down payment — potentially eliminating $15,000–$25,000 of your upfront costs. See our CHFA loans guide and down payment assistance guide.



Close near the end of the month. Prepaid interest is calculated from your closing date to month end. Closing on the 27th means 3–4 days of interest instead of 27.



Ask your lender about lender credits. A slightly higher interest rate in exchange for a lender credit toward closing costs can make sense if you're cash-limited upfront.



Shop for owner's title insurance. Title insurance premiums in Connecticut are not state-regulated at a fixed rate — you can compare pricing between title insurers, particularly on the owner's policy.

LGBTQ Couples and Title: What to Decide at Closing



Closing costs are identical regardless of sexual orientation or marital status. But there's a decision worth making before you get to the closing table: how you take title.



Connecticut offers two primary options for co-buyers:

Joint tenancy with right of survivorship: If one partner dies, ownership passes automatically to the survivor — no probate

Tenants in common: Each partner owns a defined percentage; on death, that share goes to their estate



For LGBTQ couples — especially unmarried partners — joint tenancy is often the right choice for survivorship protection. Your closing attorney will advise on your specific situation, including any estate planning considerations.

Frequently Asked Questions



What are typical closing costs in Connecticut?

Between 2% and 5% of the purchase price. On a $400,000 home, plan for $8,000–$20,000 depending on your town's mill rate, loan size, and lender fees.



Does Connecticut have a transfer tax?

Connecticut has a conveyance tax paid by the seller, not the buyer. It ranges from 1.00%–2.50% of the sale price.



Does Connecticut have a mortgage recording tax?

No. Unlike New York State, Connecticut has no mortgage recording tax. This is a significant savings for buyers relocating from NY.



Do I need an attorney to buy a home in Connecticut?

Yes. Connecticut is an attorney-closing state — a real estate attorney is required. Budget $1,000–$2,000.



Why is the property tax escrow so large?

Connecticut properties are assessed at 70% of market value and taxed at the town's mill rate. In high-rate towns (West Hartford 44.78, Hartford 68.95), the annual tax on a typical home is $12,000–$20,000+. At 9–10 months of taxes collected upfront, the escrow deposit is proportionally large. This is why choosing a lower-mill-rate town materially affects your upfront costs.



Can I roll closing costs into my mortgage?

Sometimes, depending on the loan type. CHFA's Time To Own program can also cover closing costs as a forgivable loan — see our down payment assistance guide.

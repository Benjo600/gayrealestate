# What are the typical closing costs for a home buyer in Connecticut in 2025 or 2026? Break down all itemized costs including attorney fees, title insurance, recording fees, transfer taxes, mortgage origination fees, prepaid property taxes, homeowner's insurance escrow, and any other standard Connecticut closing costs. Include typical dollar ranges and what percentage of the purchase price closing costs represent. Note any Connecticut-specific costs that differ from other states.

Typical buyer closing costs in Connecticut in 2025–2026 usually run about **2% to 5% of the purchase price**, with many lender-and-attorney estimates landing closer to around **0.9% to 1.5% for the buyer’s direct closing charges before escrows and prepaids**. Connecticut is a bit unusual because real estate closings are attorney-run, town-clerk recording fees are standardized by municipality, and the state has a separate conveyance tax structure that mainly affects sellers rather than buyers.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

## Typical buyer cost range

For a standard financed home purchase, a practical planning range is:

- **Low end:** about **2%** of purchase price, if the loan is simple and escrows are modest.[^2][^5]
- **Common range:** about **2% to 5%** of purchase price when you include lender fees, title work, prepaids, and escrow funding.[^5][^2]
- **Example:** on a **\$400,000** home, that works out to roughly **\$8,000 to \$20,000** total.[^2]

A narrower Connecticut-specific benchmark from Rocket Mortgage puts the average buyer closing cost at about **0.93% of purchase price** for lender/title/recording-type charges, but your actual cash needed at closing can be much higher once you add escrow deposits for taxes and insurance.[^1]

## Connecticut-specific items

Connecticut has a few closing-cost features that differ from many states. First, buyers generally work with a real estate attorney, and attorney fees are a normal part of closing here rather than an unusual extra. Second, recording is done through the **town clerk**, not a county recorder system, so fees can differ by municipality and document type.[^3][^11][^9][^10][^1]

Another Connecticut-specific item is the **real estate conveyance tax**, but that is generally paid by the seller, not the buyer. The state tax is tiered at **0.75%** up to \$800,000, **1.25%** from \$800,001 to \$2.5 million, and **2.25%** above \$2.5 million, with municipal conveyance taxes usually adding **0.25%** and some towns charging **0.50%**. Connecticut also does **not** add a separate mortgage tax the way some states do.[^12][^4][^13][^14]

## What buyers should budget

For budgeting, a good rule of thumb is to set aside **about 3% to 4% of the purchase price** for Connecticut closing costs if you want a realistic cushion, especially if your lender requires larger escrow deposits. On a **\$500,000** home, that means about **\$15,000 to \$20,000** in closing cash, though some buyers will come in lower and some higher depending on taxes, insurance, and loan fees.

The Best LGBTQ+-Friendly States to Live In

Moving is a big decision, and for LGBTQ+ people, it’s more than just finding the right house. It’s about knowing you’ll be treated fairly when you apply for a mortgage, among many other things.

The peace of mind that comes with legal protection is invaluable, so if you’re a first-time homebuyer or looking to relocate to a more inclusive environment, it’s essential to do some research into the most LGBTQ-friendly states.

We’ll be highlighting some of the most LGBTQ+-friendly states in the USA, with a focus on non-discrimination laws and the broader protections that make a difference for homebuyers planning a move.

We’ll cover what to look for (like housing and employment protections), and point you towards resources that can help you go deeper (including working with LGBTQ-friendly real estate professionals).

## The Most LGBTQ+-Friendly States

Let’s start with the three ‘big hitters’ that consistently stand out for statewide protections and visible LGBTQ+ communities: Massachusetts, New York, and California.

When we look for LGBTQ-friendly states that lead the nation, we prioritize those with ‘Working Toward Innovative Equality’ status from the Human Rights Campaign (HRC). Why? Because these LGBTQ+ states actively defend your rights as a member of the community.

### Massachusetts

Massachusetts has long been a pioneer of LGBTQ+ rights, famously becoming the first state to legalize same-sex marriage in 2004.

It has one of the highest proportions of LGBTQ+ adults in the US, at an [estimated 6.5% (around 356,200 people](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/LGBT-Adult-US-Pop-Dec-2023.pdf)), which helps support visible networks and affirming services across the state. In 2026, it remains one of the most gay-friendly states in the country due to its legal framework.

State law explicitly prohibits discrimination based on sexual orientation and gender identity in housing and employment.

Protections in this gay-friendly state extend to public-facing settings (like businesses and services), and that’s the foundation many homebuyers are looking for. A place where the legal framework is designed to reduce the risk of unfair treatment. If you want to know [why Massachusetts is a great state for LGBTQ people](https://www.gayrealestate.com/blog/2025/11/why-massachusetts-is-a-great-state-for-lgbtq-people), it’s because it’s filled with inclusive communities and welcoming cities.

### New York

New York pairs strong statewide protections with one of the largest LGBTQ+ populations in the country.

The [Williams Institute's](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/LGBT-Adult-US-Pop-Dec-2023.pdf) estimates place New York at 5.5% LGBTQ+ adults (around 853,600 people), which translates into a robust community infrastructure (for example: services, organizations, nightlife, and advocacy), which is why its [LGBTQ+ support groups in NYC](https://www.gayrealestate.com/blog/2025/11/lgbtq-support-groups-in-new-york-finding-community-and-resources) are paramount to the LGBTQ+ community.

It consistently ranks among the most LGBTQ-friendly states thanks to the Gender Expression Non-Discrimination Act (GENDA). So if you’re eyeing up the Big Apple, it’s worth looking into the [best gay neighborhoods in NYC](https://www.gayrealestate.com/blog/2025/11/best-gay-neighborhoods-in-nyc).

### California

California is the go-to queer-friendly state for many movers because of its large community and long-standing legal protections. It also has the largest estimated number of LGBTQ+ adults of any state: about [1,549,600 people](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/LGBT-Adult-US-Pop-Dec-2023.pdf).

California’s Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA) provides rigorous protections. It was also one of the first states to declare itself a ‘sanctuary state’ for transgender youth.

Before you pack your bags, it might be helpful to review [LGBTQ rights in California](https://www.gayrealestate.com/blog/2025/12/lgbtq-rights-in-california-what-you-should-know-before-moving) to see how state laws specifically protect your homeownership journey.

### Colorado

The Williams Institute estimates 6.8% of adults in Colorado identify as LGBT (around [294,500 people](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/LGBT-Adult-US-Pop-Dec-2023.pdf)). Colorado is consistently ranked among the best states for LGBTQ individuals because of its proactive approach to civil rights.

Colorado’s Anti-Discrimination Act (CADA) protects against discrimination in housing, employment, and public accommodations. Plus, it has one of the nation’s most comprehensive bans on conversion therapy for minors (making it one of the best states for trans people, too).

### New Jersey

New Jersey is often overlooked as an LGBTQ+- friendly state because it sits next to New York and Pennsylvania, but for LGBTQ+ families, it can be a practical, protections-first option with excellent access to major job markets.

The Williams Institute estimates 5.3% of New Jersey adults identify as LGBT (about [367,300 people](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/LGBT-Adult-US-Pop-Dec-2023.pdf)). But what makes New Jersey feel especially ‘livable’ for families?

It combines suburban and small-city housing options with statewide non-discrimination protections that explicitly cover sexual orientation and gender identity in housing.

### Vermont

Vermont was the first state to introduce civil unions, and it continues to lead with a ‘small but mighty’ approach to inclusivity. The [Williams Institute](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/LGBT-Adult-US-Pop-Dec-2023.pdf) estimates Vermont has one of the highest shares of LGBT adults at 7.4%.

It’s one of the best states for gay people because Vermont’s laws are exhaustive; they cover everything from gender-neutral markers on IDs to comprehensive housing protections.

If you value a close-knit, rural, or small-town feel, Vermont is unmatched.

### Maine

Maine is a rising star for those seeking LGBTQ states with a slower pace of life. It has solidified its status as a sanctuary for the community through consistent legislative wins over the last decade. The Maine Human Rights Act ensures that sexual orientation and gender identity are protected classes.

### Rhode Island

Rhode Island is small, but it punches above its weight as one of the best states for gay people. For families, that can mean less time “starting over” because communities and services are often easier to access. The Williams Institute estimates 6.5% of Rhode Island adults identify as LGBT (around 54,800 people)

### Connecticut

The Williams Institute states that 6.0% of Connecticut adults identify as LGBT (around 170,500 people), which makes it a strong LGBTQ-friendly state for those who want Northeast access; NYC and Boston are both within reach.

It’s also a top choice for those seeking transgender-friendly states due to its expansive healthcare protections.

### Washington DC

Technically not a state, but Washington, DC is often the exception to ‘state-by-state’ lists, and it’s worth including because it has long-standing, local-level protections that many LGBTQ+ buyers find reassuring.

The DC Office of Human Rights makes discrimination illegal across multiple areas, including employment and other key settings, and it explicitly covers a wide range of protected traits.

DC can be a strong fit if you want a highly connected job market and a jurisdiction where anti-discrimination protections are treated as a baseline; it’s often featured in discussions about the [best gay cities in the USA](https://www.gayrealestate.com/blog/2026/01/best-gay-cities-in-the-usa-where-to-buy-live).

### Oregon

Oregon continues the West Coast pattern many movers look for in LGBTQ-friendly states: a visible community plus clear protections. For example, Portland earned a perfect score of 100 on the HRC’s 2024 Municipal Equality Index scorecard, reflecting a strong municipal track record on non-discrimination protections.

### Minnesota

Minnesota is one of the most important queer-friendly states in the Midwest, and the data reinforces that.

In the 2024 HRC Municipal Equality Index, Minneapolis received a final score of 100, and Saint Paul received a score of 100. That matters because municipal scorecards capture the practical side of equality. If city services and community-facing practices help LGBTQ+ residents feel supported, that’s a major factor to consider.

### Illinois

Illinois is another essential Midwest anchor for gay-friendly states, and it’s a great example of why looking at both state protections and city-level policies can give buyers more confidence.

On the municipal side, the City of Chicago earned a final score of 100 on the 2024 HRC Municipal Equality Index scorecard. That’s a concrete indicator that local non-discrimination measures and leadership are in place in ways that affect day-to-day life, including the realities of renting and buying while you settle into a new, gay-friendly community.

## What to Look for when Researching LGBTQ+-Friendly States to Live in

When you’re comparing LGBTQ-friendly states, start with what’s written into law, because legal protections are what you can rely on even when you’re new to a community.

Key protections to look for at the state level include explicit non-discrimination coverage for sexual orientation and gender identity in:

- Housing
- Employment
- Public accommodations

A good way to check for LGBTQ+-friendly states is the Movement Advancement Project (MAP) equality maps, which track state non-discrimination laws by category.

### Legal Protection for the LGBTQ+ Community

The most essential factor is the presence of LGBTQ+ and transgender-friendly states and non-discrimination laws. You want to live in a state where sexual orientation and gender identity are explicitly named as protected classes in housing and employment.

These laws prevent a landlord or employer from legally discriminating against you. Understanding [what LGBTQ housing discrimination is](https://www.gayrealestate.com/blog/2025/11/what-is-lgbtq-housing-discrimination) can help you identify red flags early in your search.

### State-Friendliness and Healthcare

‘Friendly’ in this context means you can access everyday essentials, including healthcare. Look for states with shield laws that protect gender-affirming care and reproductive rights.

### Family Recognition

For couples and families moving to states that are LGBTQ+-friendly, it helps to separate these two questions:

- Is marriage legal in the place I’m moving?
- Will my marriage be recognized everywhere?

Same-sex marriage has been legal nationwide since Obergefell vs Hodges (2015).

The Respect for Marriage Act (2022) requires states and the federal government to recognize valid same-sex marriages performed elsewhere (even if a state were to stop issuing new licenses).

This doesn’t replace the need to check practical family-related policies at the state level (like how parentage is handled and how welcoming local systems feel), but it does provide a meaningful baseline for recognition.

### Inclusive Neighborhoods

A state might be progressive, but what about daily life? Look for LGBT-friendly states to live in that score highly on the HRC Municipal Equality Index, even if state laws are still catching up. From the [best gay neighborhoods in NYC](https://www.gayrealestate.com/blog/2025/11/best-gay-neighborhoods-in-nyc) to the [top 16 gayest cities in the US](https://www.gayrealestate.com/blog/2025/11/top-16-gayest-cities-in-the-us-for-lgbtq-relocation), finding your tribe matters just as much as the legal fine print.

## FAQs

To help you on your journey, we’ve included some frequently asked questions:

### Is Gay Marriage Legal in all States?

Yes. Same-sex marriage has been legal nationwide since the US Supreme Court’s Obergefell v Hodges decision in 2015.

### What States are LGBTQ+-Friendly?

LGBTQ-friendly can mean different things, but for homebuying, the best definition is: states with explicit protections in housing and employment, plus a broader environment that supports LGBTQ+ people.

### Is Georgia LGBTQ+-friendly?

Georgia has LGBTQ+ communities and welcoming, gay-friendly pockets (especially in and around larger cities), but it does not offer full statewide non-discrimination protections across housing.

### What States are Friendlier for Trans People?

The most trans-friendly states include California, Minnesota, Washington, and New York.

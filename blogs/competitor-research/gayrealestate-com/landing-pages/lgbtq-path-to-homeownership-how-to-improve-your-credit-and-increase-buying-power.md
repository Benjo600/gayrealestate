For many in the LGBTQ community, homeownership isn’t just about building equity—it’s about establishing safety, stability, and a place to truly call your own. But even with growing access and visibility, barriers still exist, especially when it comes to financing.

If you're dreaming of owning a home but aren’t sure where to start financially, your credit score is a powerful place to begin. A strong credit profile not only opens the door to loan approvals but also increases your buying power—giving you access to better interest rates, more favorable terms, and the confidence to make informed decisions.

Here’s a step-by-step guide tailored for LGBTQ homebuyers on improving credit and strengthening your path to homeownership.

**Why Credit Matters for LGBTQ Homebuyers**

Your credit score is one of the key factors lenders use to determine:

- If you qualify for a mortgage
- How much you’ll be approved for
- What interest rate you’ll pay

Even a small difference in your credit score can significantly impact your monthly mortgage payment and the total cost of your loan. For LGBTQ buyers, who may face unique financial hurdles such as student debt, income disparities, or discrimination in lending, it’s especially important to take control of your credit early in the process.

**1\. Know Where You Stand**

Start by pulling your credit report from all three major bureaus—Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. You can do this for free once a year at AnnualCreditReport.com.

Check for:

- Incorrect or outdated information
- Duplicate accounts
- Late payments or accounts in collections

If you spot errors, dispute them immediately. Even a single correction can boost your score.

**2\. Pay Down High-Interest Debt**

Credit utilization—how much of your available credit you’re using—is a major factor in your score. Aim to keep credit card balances below 30% of your credit limit, and ideally closer to 10%.

Start with high-interest credit cards and work your way down. Setting up a payment plan or using a debt snowball strategy (paying off smallest debts first) can help build momentum.

**3\. Make On-Time Payments Non-Negotiable**

Late or missed payments can stay on your credit report for up to seven years. Set up automatic payments or reminders to ensure you never miss a due date.

Consistent, on-time payments demonstrate financial reliability to lenders.

**4\. Don’t Close Old Accounts (Unless Necessary)**

The age of your credit accounts also impacts your score. Even if you’re not using an old credit card, keeping it open can help your credit history and overall utilization.

Only close accounts if there are high fees or legitimate concerns about identity theft.

**5\. Consider a Credit-Builder Loan or Secured Credit Card**

If you’re new to credit or rebuilding from past challenges, consider tools like:

- **Secured credit cards**, where you provide a deposit as collateral
- **Credit-builder loans**, which let you build payment history while saving money

These are especially helpful for LGBTQ individuals who may have avoided traditional financial institutions in the past due to bias or lack of access.

**6\. Avoid Large Financial Changes Before You Buy**

Once you’re preparing to apply for a mortgage, avoid:

- Opening new credit accounts
- Taking on new loans (like a car loan)
- Making large purchases on credit

These changes can lower your score or raise red flags with lenders.

**7\. Work with LGBTQ-Affirming Professionals**

These agents can help:

- Navigate neighborhood selection with inclusivity in mind
- Offer support in cases of financial discrimination

**Homeownership Is Within Reach**

Improving your credit isn’t about perfection—it’s about progress. With the right steps and support, you can increase your buying power and take meaningful steps toward owning a home that reflects who you are.
